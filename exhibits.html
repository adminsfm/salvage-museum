Explore the Exhibits

Follow the story from the lobby into the original 1860s house, then upstairs into family, craft, and community life.

Welcome and Salvage History

Begin with the welcome panel, historic area maps, information on the Lane/Heffern family, and the story of Salvage as a central fishing community in Bonavista Bay.

The Crystal Stream

Learn about schooners, the Hunter family, the fishing trade, and the vessels that carried people, supplies, news, and fish products through outport Newfoundland.

Photo to add

The Lane/Heffern House

Explore the timeline and architecture of the house, including exposed wooden walls, the stone fireplace, the story of the families who lived here.

Women in the Home

Household tools, lighting, heating, foodways, textiles, and family labour demonstrate how women's work extended far beyond the walls of the house.

1969: The House Becomes a Museum

This exhibit highlights the community museum tradition of donated and loaned household items, work tools, records, and objects residents felt should be preserved.

Skills and Crafts

See local handmade objects and traditions including spinning, knitting, crocheting sewing, broom making, fence-making, woodworking, and other talents.

Heber Heffern: Broom Man of Salvage

Photographs and examples of handmade birch brooms tell the story of a local craft now recognized as an endangered tradition in Newfoundland and Labrador.

Times in the Parlour: Music, Stories, and Radio

The parlour features songbooks, musical instruments, vintage radios, excerpts from the Doyle Bulletin, Confederation-era change, and the legacy of Gerald S. Doyle. 

Annie Lane and NONIA

Learn about the Newfoundland Outport Nursing Industrial Association through Annie Lane, who knit for NONIA for over 70 years.

Bessie's Sewing Closet

The under-stair closet remembers Bessie Heffern's sewing nook through her original sewing machine alongside textiles. It also features poetry written by the women of Salvage.

Photo to add

From the Cradle to the Grave

Upstairs, a replica of a bedroom featuring a wooden cradle, clothing, hats, jewellery, a feather bed, and the Shroud of Salvage speak to family life and death customs.

Community Institutions

The Bishop's Harbour room and hallway include artifacts and archives on schools, church organizations, commercial activity, hunting and fishing, leisure, the Fisherman's Protective Union, and the Loyal Orange Lodge.

Self-Guided Tour

A self-guided tour is available at the museum. Staff and volunteers are happy to answer questions, help with church records or archival resources, and point visitors toward exhibits that match their interests.
